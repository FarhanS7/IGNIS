The Evidence Engine & Trust Model

Every insight in IGNIS carries an explicit authority level. We distinguish directly observed NASA raw telemetry from synthetic AI summaries.

Level A
Observed / NASA Source

Directly reported or recorded in NASA flight data, PSI archives, or peer-reviewed literature. Maximum authority.

Level B
Normalized Source Data

Structured, standardized, and unit-converted data tables with verified DOI and NASA technical report accession numbers.

Level C
Derived by IGNIS

Computed scientific metrics such as Computer Vision flame area, growth curves, and centroid tracking. Fully reproducible.

Level D
Similarity Interpretation

Inference derived by comparing multi-dimensional environmental vectors with transparent mathematical weighting.

Level E
AI Synthesis

Natural language explanations generated by LLM strictly constrained to retrieved source passages. Zero hallucination.

Physics Fundamentals • P0

Why Does Fire Burn as a Sphere in Space?

On Earth, hot air rises because it is lighter than cold air. In zero gravity, buoyancy disappears. Without buoyancy, hot combustion gases do not rise — the flame surrounds the fuel in a serene, blue spherical halo.

Ventilation Dynamics • P1

The Airflow Paradox in Spacecraft

Without ventilation, a microgravity flame quickly suffocates in its own carbon dioxide. A gentle breeze from the life support system (0.05 m/s) actually feeds the flame by replenishing oxygen.

GROUNDED ANSWER • 94% EVIDENCE COVERAGE
In Earth gravity, hot combustion gases rise due to buoyancy (natural convection), pulling fresh oxygen into the base and stretching the flame into an elongated tear-drop shape. In microgravity (0g), gravity-driven buoyant convection is absent. Oxygen transport occurs almost purely via molecular diffusion, causing the reaction zone to form a symmetrical sphere around the fuel source with significantly lower flame temperatures and a distinct blue chemiluminescent glow.
Evidence Sources: NASA TM-2015-218820 (FLEX-2) ISS Physical Science Informatics (PSI) Saffire-I Flight Report
