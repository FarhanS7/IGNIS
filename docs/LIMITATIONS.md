# Known Engineering Limitations & Scientific Scope Boundaries

This document provides a comprehensive, transparent audit of the physical, algorithmic, and operational boundaries of the IGNIS Fire Response Console (`/response`) and associated machine learning models.

---

## 1. Simulated Sensors vs Physical Hardware

The sensor reading streams ingested by `/response` are generated by an in-browser 1D compartment transport model rather than physical hardware sensors mounted inside a physical habitat.
- **Assumptions**: Zone compartments are modeled as well-mixed volumes with uniform concentration across each zone.
- **Physical Reality**: Real spacecraft compartments experience complex micro-recirculation eddies, duct boundary layers, filter loading, and localized stagnation pockets.
- **Operational Boundary**: The simulator is designed for conceptual response protocol verification, educational exploration, and design-consistency checking; it is not a CFD (Computational Fluid Dynamics) engine.

---

## 2. Earth-Gravity Training Data for M2 Sensor Fusion

The M2 multi-sensor fusion detector (`trained:m2-fusion-v1`) was trained on open-access ground laboratory experiments from Mendeley Data:
- **Datasets**: EN54 Test Room (D1, DOI `10.17632/npk2zcm85h.2`) and Industrial Hall (D2, DOI `10.17632/yghykzm4km.1`).
- **Domain Shift**: On Earth ($1\,g$), buoyant thermal plumes rapidly transport heat, carbon monoxide, and smoke particles upward toward ceiling sensors. In microgravity ($\mu g$), buoyant convection is absent ($Gr \approx 0$); particulate transport is entirely governed by forced ventilation airspeeds and slow molecular diffusion.
- **Mitigation & Honest Label**: M2 uses 10 unit-free statistical features (normalized ratios, window slopes, max-to-mean ratios) to improve transferability, but **has not been validated on real microgravity fire telemetry**. The console prominently displays: *"Trained on Earth-gravity data. Not validated in microgravity."*

---

## 3. Droplet-Only Extinction Evidence for M4

The M4 flame extinction model (`trained:m4-extinction-v1`) is trained on NASA Physical Sciences Informatics PSI-69 (FLEX) data:
- **Physical Regime**: Isolated liquid fuel droplets (methanol and heptane, initial diameters $1.5\text{--}4.5\,\text{mm}$) burning symmetrically in a quiescent, sealed combustion chamber aboard the ISS Combustion Integrated Rack (CIR).
- **Domain Limitation**: **Droplet flames are physically distinct from solid spacecraft materials.** Solid materials (e.g., Kapton wire insulation, Nomex fabric, PMMA, silicone gaskets) burn via surface pyrolysis, fuel charring, and complex solid-phase kinetics.
- **Experimental Proof**: Leave-One-Fuel-Out (LOFO) validation across methanol and heptane causes the False Extinction Rate to surge to 41.9%–76.5%, demonstrating that flammability boundaries cannot be extrapolated even between simple hydrocarbon liquid fuel classes, let alone to solid polymers.
- **Operational Boundary**: M4 informs astronauts and operators of fundamental physical chemical regime boundaries; it **never automatically triggers fire suppression**.

---

## 4. No Partial-Gravity Smoke Transport Validation

While the habitat graph includes lunar surface habitat presets, partial-gravity smoke transport ($0.166\,g$ lunar, $0.38\,g$ martian) is **unvalidated**:
- **Physical Reason**: Fractional gravity regimes introduce weak, non-linear buoyancy forces that compete directly with forced ventilation airflow. Particulate settling rates, plume tilt, and ceiling jet stratification differ from both terrestrial $1\,g$ and orbital $\mu g$.
- **Console Behavior**: Selecting a lunar habitat preset immediately renders an unvalidated partial-gravity advisory and forces the smoke localization status to `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`.

---

## 5. Zone-Level Localization Only

The time-of-arrival localization algorithm (`localizer.ts`) estimates source locations at the **zone level** (e.g. Node 1, Lab Module, Core Bay, Airlock):
- **Granularity**: The algorithm ranks discrete zones based on inter-detector onset arrival delays.
- **Limitation**: It cannot pinpoint specific equipment racks, sub-rack avionics chassis, or cable bundles within a zone.
- **Ambiguity Guard**: When arrival time differences between adjacent zones fall below the travel threshold ($\Delta t < 2.0\,\text{s}$), the status is explicitly classified as `AMBIGUOUS` rather than guessing.

---

## 6. Pre-Ignition Heated Materials in NASA SAME / SAME-R

The M1 detector response calibration (`calibrated:m1-same-r`) uses data from NASA SAME (PSI-102) and SAME-R (PSI-101):
- **Experimental Setup**: Solid material samples (Teflon, Kapton, Silicone, Lampwick, Pyrell) were electrically heated to generate smoldering and pyrolysis aerosols in controlled flow ducts.
- **Limitation**: These experiments measured pre-ignition aerosol particle size distributions; they did not measure catastrophic high-heat-release flaming combustion.
- **Operational Boundary**: Model predictions are valid solely within the empirical aerodynamic mean diameter range ($0.049\,\mu\text{m} \le d_{\text{AMD}} \le 1.103\,\mu\text{m}$). Outside this domain, the model clamps and flags inputs as out-of-bounds.

---

## 7. Sample Size Constraints

Spaceflight combustion experiments are rare, costly, and resource-constrained:
- **NASA SAME / SAME-R (M1)**: 130 usable flight runs across 5 material classes.
- **NASA FLEX / FLEX-2 (M4)**: 262 usable droplet runs across 82 physical mixture groups.
- **NASA BASS-II (Evidence Base)**: 132 usable solid burns across 110 independent hardware groups.
- **Consequence**: Small sample counts require rigorous grouped cross-validation (Grouped LOOCV, LOMO, group-disjoint conformal calibration) and bootstrap confidence intervals. High-capacity deep neural networks would severely overfit; IGNIS strictly restricts ML models to regularized linear classifiers with conformal uncertainty sets.

---

## 8. Monte Carlo Sweep is a Design-Consistency Check

The built-in Monte Carlo validation tool on `/response` evaluates 100 to 500 stochastic scenarios across randomized fire growth rates, peak heat release rates, fuel types, and nuisance dust injection:
- **What it Evaluates**: Whether detector arms and response sequencing logic exhibit numerically stable, consistent behavior without logic deadlocks, divide-by-zero crashes, or uncontrolled false alarm spikes.
- **What it Does NOT Evaluate**: It does **not** prove microgravity operational superiority. Both the detector models and the simulator share synthetic assumptions.
- **Honest Label**: The results table explicitly states: *"behaves sensibly on simulator streams; both model and simulator carry assumptions; not a superiority claim"*.

## 9. Non-Operational & Educational Disclaimer

IGNIS is an interactive educational and mission architecture research tool developed for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026.
- **Not Flight Software**: IGNIS is **not** certified flight software.
- **No Safety Clearance**: Outputs do **not** constitute operational mission safety clearance, flight qualification, or certified life-support compliance under **NASA-STD-6001B** or **SSP 50005**.
- **Human In The Loop**: In all crewed operational modes, life-critical fire suppression commands strictly require human astronaut confirmation.
